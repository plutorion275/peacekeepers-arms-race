RQ2 · Weapon-Class Lead-Lag — Case File

A textbook test said 8 of 12. The correct null says zero.

Do specific weapon-class acquisitions — aircraft & drones, missiles, naval, ground systems — precede low-intensity conflict within a short window? This section is the project's central methodological contribution: not a single statistic, but a five-stage audit trail where each stage revises the one before it.

Stage A · The Original Test

Dumitrescu–Hurlin panel Granger causality, run textbook-standard

Twelve pre-specified cells — four weapon classes × three conflict outcomes — tested at a one-year lag, significance assessed against the conventional within-country shuffle null. The result looks striking: 8 of 12 cells flagged significant. The strongest signal is ground-systems deliveries preceding war-level participation.

Stage B · The Discovery

The shuffle null erases a real feature of the data

The treatment series — a country's year-to-year weapon deliveries — has genuine negative lag-1 autocorrelation in the observed data (−0.33): a big delivery year tends to be followed by a leaner one, consistent with how procurement tranches actually arrive. A random within-country shuffle destroys almost all of that structure, dropping the shuffled series' autocorrelation to −0.02 — indistinguishable from a random walk. The null the test is calibrated against no longer resembles the real data it's supposed to stand in for.

−0.33Real series autocorrelation
−0.02Shuffled series autocorrelation

Stage C · The Correction

Circular-shift null: same rotation, real structure intact

Instead of randomly reordering each country's treatment values, each series is rotated by a random offset — preserving its own autocorrelation almost exactly (−0.31 vs. −0.33 observed) while still fully destroying temporal alignment with the outcome. Rerunning the identical test 2,000 times against this correctly-specified null — where by construction no relationship exists — it still rejects at conventional significance 32–48% of the time across the twelve cells: six to ten times its own nominal 5% rate. That is the calibrated size of the naive test on this exact data.

−0.31Circular-shift autocorrelation
32–48%False-positive rate (nominal: 5%)

Stage D · Multiple-Testing Correction

Benjamini–Hochberg across the 12-cell family: zero survive

Applying FDR correction across all twelve pre-specified cells against the corrected null produces a starkly different picture. The strongest raw signal — ground systems preceding war participation — has a per-cell permutation p of .032 in isolation, nominally still under .05, but a BH-adjusted q-value of .390 once the full family is accounted for. No other cell comes closer.

0 / 12Cells survive BH correction
q=.390Best surviving cell (Ground→War)

Stage E · Independent Corroboration

An out-of-sample forecasting benchmark reaches the same answer, by a different route

A rolling-origin design asks a decision question directly: does knowing a country's weapon-class deliveries actually improve real forecasts of next-year conflict-participation change, beyond an AR(1)-only baseline using the country's own recent history? Forecast-skill differential is close to zero and inconsistently signed across all twelve cells — from a 0.02% improvement to a 0.06% degradation. Tested against the same circular-shift null with the same BH correction: zero survivors, independently.

Verdict

Not "the drone effect is false" — a narrower, more honest claim

Within a one-to-three-year window, at four broad weapon-category aggregates, there is no detectable lead-lag signal under any of three independent tests. That doesn't rule out the underlying mechanism operating on a longer horizon, at a finer weapon taxonomy, or through conflict types — cyber operations, sub-threshold strikes — that UCDP's battle-death floor doesn't capture at all.

The methodological lesson generalises well beyond this dataset: any panel series with genuine negative serial correlation — procurement cycles, discrete-tranche deliveries — is a candidate for the same shuffle-null miscalibration, silently, unless checked directly.
