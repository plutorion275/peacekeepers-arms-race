RQ3 · Country Archetypes

Four archetypes. Predictive across all four. Stable in only two.

K-Means clustering on 610 country-epoch observations (157 countries × four decade-epochs) recovers four data-driven conflict-capability archetypes — matching the number the project's theoretical framing anticipated. Most countries aren't fixed to one archetype: 100 of 157 (64%) appear in more than one across the four epochs studied.

How Four Was Chosen

One criterion says 4. Another says 8. Both are reported.

Silhouette score peaks at k=4 (0.293) across the tested range k=2–8. The gap statistic instead favors k=8, still rising at the boundary of what was tested — and Ward hierarchical clustering, run independently on the identical feature set, shows the same disagreement in its own right (Ward's own silhouette also maximizes at k=8). k=4 is used as the primary specification because silhouette is the more robust criterion for the non-uniform, regionally clustered distribution typical of country panels — a documented disagreement, not an unambiguous answer.

Stability vs. Predictive Validity

Two different questions — and they don't have to agree

Bootstrap stability asks whether the same partition reappears on a different sample. Out-of-sample validation asks whether membership carries real forward-looking information regardless of reproducibility. Held out against 2019–2024 conflict data with clusters trained on pre-2019 features only, cluster membership strongly predicts future conflict — including for the two archetypes whose bootstrap stability falls short.

Countries Don't Stay Put

64% of countries appear in more than one archetype across the four epochs

Direct, epoch-level evidence for a "soft regions on a continuum" reading, rather than fixed types assumed without support.

Verdict

Predictive, partially stable — reported as a genuine split

Safe Hegemon and Inert Non-Combatant are the more durable categories; Defensive Deterrent and Armed Instabilizer are better read as real, predictively useful, but softer regions on a continuum than sharply bounded types. A paper reporting four uniformly "stable" clusters under the conventional 0.75 threshold would have been reporting an artefact of the threshold, not a finding about the world.
