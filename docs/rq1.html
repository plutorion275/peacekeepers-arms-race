RQ1 · Compositional Shift

The substitution hypothesis is not supported. It's amplification.

If capability bought states a substitution from war toward minor conflict, the war-to-minor ratio should fall with capability, and extraterritorial engagement should rise. Two-way fixed-effects panel regression — country and year fixed effects, controls for GDP, population, and regime score, standard errors clustered by country — finds neither. It finds more of both, and above all, deadlier conflict once it occurs.

The Headline Result

Not composition. Lethality.

The single strongest result in the entire project isn't one of the five compositional outcomes at all. Battle-death intensity (log-transformed battle-related deaths) rises sharply and significantly with capability — and a leave-one-out jackknife re-estimating the regression 193 times, once per country removed, keeps the coefficient positive and significant in every single case, ranging narrowly between 1.82 and 2.16.

Mechanism Checks

Is the lethality effect broad, or an artefact of one region or regime type?

Two independent probes ask whether the battle-death finding is diffuse or driven by a subgroup — both point toward a broad-based effect that is regionally concentrated but not regime-contingent.

Reading Caution

Intensity co-moves with conflict — treat it as associational

A lagged-MTS check finds the battle-death association attenuates sharply once capability is measured before rather than during a conflict-year: contemporaneous β=2.03*** falls to β=0.97** at t−1 and to a null β=−0.01 by t−3, a 100% drop. Capability co-moves with, and may partly respond to, ongoing conflict — which is why every claim on this page is reported as associational, not causal.

Verdict

Amplification, not substitution

Higher-capability states are not trading war for minor conflict — they participate in more of both, and above all, their conflicts are measurably deadlier once they occur. That's a different theoretical claim than Snyder's original compositional formulation: closer to capability lowering the threshold for entering any conflict than to capability specifically redirecting states toward the cheapest kind.
